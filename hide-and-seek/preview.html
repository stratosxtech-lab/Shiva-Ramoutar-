The commercial case

What it could earn, and what it could sell for

On its own, an app idea rarely sells for much, because buyers pay for proof: a working product, users and revenue. The value grows sharply at each stage below, so the best way to increase the price is to build and launch it.

1 · Pricing to customers

PlanPriceWhat it includes
Free$0Classic mode, up to 6 players, ads between rounds
Premium$4.99 / moor $29.99 / yrAll modes, up to 20 players, avatars, seasonal events, no ads

Teams: per event$750 – $2,500Up to 150 players, branded arena, organiser dashboard and report
Teams: annual$6,000 – $24,000Unlimited events, onboarding mode, HR analytics, priority support

2 · Sale value of the business, by stage

StageTypical rangeHow buyers value it

B2B Teams with recurring revenue3× – 6× ARRExample: 30 company clients at $10k = $300k ARR → about $0.9M – $1.8M

What I can build with you

  • The full app code: iOS and Android from one codebase (React Native / Expo)
  • The backend: live location sharing, game rooms, scoring and accounts
  • Game logic: warm zones, chirps, QR tagging, all five modes
  • Teams dashboard: organiser tools and leadership reports for the web
  • Launch materials: landing page, store listing copy, privacy policy draft

What you would provide

  • Developer accounts: Apple ($99 a year) and Google Play ($25 one-time)
  • Field testing: real people playing outdoors to tune GPS accuracy
  • Legal review: location data and children's privacy (COPPA, GDPR)
  • Running costs: hosting and maps, about $200 – $1,500 a month at an early scale
  • For comparison: an agency would quote about $80k – $180k and 4 – 6 months for this MVP

The figures are planning estimates based on typical pricing for mobile games and team-building software, and on typical multiples for small app and SaaS acquisitions. Actual values depend on traction, retention, revenue quality and market conditions.
